Rules that apply everywhere here (2026)

Bass: 42 cm minimum · 3 per angler per day · catch-and-release ONLY 1 Feb – 31 Mar. Eels: always released — protected. Dee Estuary is a statutory Bass Nursery Area (31 May–1 Oct): shore anglers are exempt from the boat ban but expected to release bass voluntarily in that window. No EA rod licence for sea species from the shore; a licence IS needed to target salmon/sea trout anywhere — any accidental migratory fish goes straight back. Rules are reviewed annually — re-check NWIFCA/gov.uk before a trip.

The spine of everything: the running ledger. The lead anchors; the line slides through the link so a fish never feels the weight; the fluoro section is deliberately the weakest so snags cost a hook, not the rig. Every bait session on this guide is a variation of this one chain.

Phase 1 — At home, the night beforeKnots, hooklengths, packing. Do the fiddly work in the warm.

1

Assemble the rod for practice

Sections join with a push and a quarter-twist — snug, never forced. Sight down the rod and line up the guides. Fit the short tip for bait in tide, the regular tip for lures and slack water. Mount the reel, hand-tight.

2

Check the braid

Pull a few metres off: it should sit firm on the spool (fingernail press = solid, not spongy), no fuzzing or fading, and survive a hard pull between gloved hands. Loose braid? Walk it out 150 m under tension and rewind firmly.

3

Learn the one knot: the uni

It does every connection in this guide — braid to swivel, fluoro to hook, leader to lure. Practise five times at the table on the mono spool. Always wet it before pulling tight; braid burns itself dry.

Watch: uni knot
4

Pre-tie 3–4 hooklengths

50 cm of fluoro → uni knot to an Aberdeen → slide 2–3 bright beads down to the hook → uni knot a size-6 swivel on the top end. Wind each onto a strip of card. On a windy shore this preparation is the difference between fishing and fumbling.

5

Check the tide, then pack

Pick a neap tide (the small ones — any tide app shows the range) and plan to fish from ~2 hours before high water through the top. Pack: leads, hooklengths, spare hooks & swivels, bait elastic, pliers, scissors, towel, headtorch, bucket, water, and ragworm in a cool bag — collected fresh that day.

Bait note: ragworm bites — grip it behind the head. Backup if worm falls through: supermarket raw prawns or a Gulp Sandworm.

Phase 2 — At the waterRig, bait, cast, read the tip, land the fish.

6

Set up above the tide line

Flat ground, gear behind you, and clock your exit route before you fish. On estuary sand, the water comes to you.

7

Build the running ledger

Exactly as the big diagram above: thread the snap-link up the braid (it must slide) → thread one small bead → uni knot the braid to a swivel → clip on a pre-tied hooklength → clip a 1 oz (28 g) grip lead into the sliding link. One knot tied on site; everything else was done at the kitchen table.

Lead maths: 28 g lead + rig + bait ≈ 40 g — right at the rod's ceiling with the short tip. Slack water: swap to a 15–20 g flat lead and let it trundle. If 28 g won't hold, fish the margins — don't up the lead.
8

Bait: worm up the shank

Watch: baiting ragworm
9

Set the drag, then cast

Drag first: turn the knob on the spool face until pulling line off by hand takes firm-but-yielding pressure. Braid has no stretch — the drag is your only shock absorber.

Watch: casting a spinning reel
10

Read the tip, wait out the rattle

Wind slowly until you feel solid weight, then lift the rod firmly. No dramatic strike — the thin-wire Aberdeen sets itself. Wind steadily and slide the fish up the sand or lift it over the margin.

11

Land, unhook, release (or keep)

Wet your hands first. Lay a flounder flat, grip gently across the body, back the hook out the way it came — pliers for anything deep (eels especially). Going back: hold it upright in the shallows until it kicks. Keeping one: dispatch immediately with a firm knock to the head, and know the size rules — bass minimum is 42 cm with strict bag limits; undersized anything goes back. Your rod bag has a printed measure.

Watch: unhooking flatfish
12

The rhythm

Fresh worm every 15–20 minutes, touched or not — washed-out bait stops working. Nothing in an hour? Move 30–50 m. Flounder are patchy: finding them beats waiting for them.

L

Lure mode (swaps for steps 7–10)

Fit the regular tip. Uni knot a metre of fluoro to the braid, uni knot an 18 g metal to that. Cast, sink for a 3–5 count, retrieve steady-medium with pauses. A hit is a dead stop or a thump — just keep winding. Shads on 7–10 g jig heads: same connection, bounced slowly along channel edges at dawn and dusk for bass.

Phase 3 — HomeTwo minutes that doubles the reel's life.

13

Rinse, dry, relax the drag

Gentle fresh-water rinse of rod and reel (never dunk or jet-wash — that drives salt into the bearings), wipe dry, back the drag knob right off, and let the rod sections dry apart before they go back in the tube. Salt-treated is not salt-proof.

The whole guide in one line: assemble, one knot everywhere, worm up the shank, lob it, watch the tip, wait out the rattle, lift, wet hands, rinse the reel.

Tide safety — non-negotiable

  • Duddon & Mersey sands flood fast and fill channels behind you. Fish from the edges; never wander the flats on a making tide.
  • Perch Rock causeway cuts off early on the flood. Know your return time before you walk out.
  • Check tide times every session. Start backing off as soon as the flood begins.
  • Greasy rocks + running tide = fish from the beach or solid standings, not the outer rocks.
  • Tell someone where you are. Take the headtorch even for “daylight” sessions.
  • Weever fish sting: hot water immersion (~40–45°C, 30+ min), remove visible spines, seek help if it worsens. Wear footwear on wet sand.
  • Discarded line kills wildlife (and dogs). Every scrap of trimmings goes in a pocket, home, and cut into pieces before the bin.

🐾 Dog rules at the mark

  • Baited hooks never touch the ground. A hook in rag smells like dinner; a swallowed hook is an emergency vet job. Rig in hand or in the bucket, always.
  • Bait bucket stays lidded — a tub of ragworm is irresistible and expensive to replace.
  • Clip the dog on while casting and while a fish is on — a dog lunging at a flapping flounder mid-unhook is how hooks end up in paws and lips.
  • Check paws before leaving: hooks, glass, and weever spines all hide in tideline sand.
  • Estuary mud that takes your boot takes a dog too — same rule for both of you: stay off the soft flats.
  • Salt water drunk from the shallows = a sick dog in the tent later. Bring fresh water and offer it often.
